I developed a driver-assisted stair-climbing platform (for wheelchairs and other applications) built around a 4WD articulated chassis with a passive yaw joint. The vehicle is capable of slipless maneuvering down to a 1 m turn radius. Tighter turns are possible with a skid-steer mode (unified under a single control interface). The seat is mounted on a self-leveling mechanism that keeps the seat horizontal while climbing or descending stairs. A driver-assist system allows for easy stair operation (keeps the vehicle square with the steps) and avoids collisions with obstacles.

I built this entire project from scratch. This includes mechanical design, fabrication, integration, ROS 2 software architecture, control systems, and computer vision.

Off-the-shelf components include: onboard computer, motors and motor controllers, camera, IMU, encoders, and electrical components.

### Hardware upgrades

I am in the process of doing a number of hardware upgrades:
- Wheels: the original wheels had uneven radial stiffness, which made for a bumpy ride and poor handling. The new wheels have proven to be a significant upgrade for higher speed driving and general smoothness.
- Seat: the original seat was a quick proof of concept, meant to be replaced, which finally happened! Armrests are on the way.
- Linear actuator: the current solution with the rotary actuator has poor rigidity (largely from the gear train). I will be testing a linear actuator to allow for higher overall chair stiffness and a self-locking mechanism, for safety and power efficiency.
- Onboard controls: addition of a joystick and touch screen for full control and awareness by the user.
