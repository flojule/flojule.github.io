## Overview

Flowheely is a semi-autonomous stair-climbing wheelchair built around a 4WD articulated chassis with a passive yaw joint between front and rear bodies. The yaw joint lets the chassis curve around turning stairs while keeping all four soft airless wheels in driven contact with the steps.

The full-scale platform is in mechanical and electrical integration. Kinematics and the control architecture were first validated on a 1/4-scale test vehicle (miniwheely), described below.

## Control

All three control modes close the loop on the yaw-joint angle between front and rear bodies. Mode selection determines what the operator (or planner) commands; the joint controller and the wheel-speed allocation handle the rest.

- **Differential drive (joint locked at 0&deg;)** — front and rear bodies are held collinear and the wheelchair behaves as a rigid 4WD differential-drive robot. Used for tight maneuvers and general navigation, manual or autonomous.
- **Yaw-angle steering** — the operator commands a target joint angle. Feels like driving a car: the joint sets a steady-state turn radius and the wheels are coordinated to follow it. Manual driving mode.
- **Body yaw-rate control** — the controller takes a commanded body yaw rate and resolves it through the joint and wheel speeds. Used for autonomous navigation.

The articulated yaw joint is the key enabler for curved stair climbing; whether to also use it on flat ground (versus pure differential drive) is still TBD.

## Miniwheely (1/4-scale)

Miniwheely is a 1/4-scale platform built to validate the kinematics and the articulated-chassis concept before committing to the full-scale build. The original goal also included validating stair climbing, but building a representative scaled stair setup turned out to be more involved than the validation was worth. Once the control architecture was understood and working on miniwheely, moving to the full-scale platform was the right next step.
